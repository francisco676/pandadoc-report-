I don't run my team on a sales methodology. I run it on a philosophy. And it comes from a book most people don't associate with revenue teams: The Four Agreements by Don Miguel Ruiz. If you haven't read it, it sounds like a self-help book. And it is. But it's also the best framework for high-performance sales culture I've ever found. Here's how I apply it: 1️⃣ Don't make assumptions. The fastest way to lose a deal is to think you already know what the customer needs. 2️⃣ Always do your best. Not "do more." Do your best with what you have today. 3️⃣ Be impeccable with your word. In sales, trust is everything. If you say you'll follow up by Thursday, follow up by Thursday. 4️⃣ Don't take anything personally. The prospect who ghosts you isn't rejecting you. They're busy, scared, or just not ready. The best sales cultures I've been part of were built on curiosity and trust. Not scripts and pressure. Which of the four do you find hardest to practice at work? 👇

Story time: I joined a new company after 12 years at the same place. Within a week, I started changing things around, feeling an urge to immediately add value. I was trying to help make things better for the people. (At least, that's the story I was telling myself.) What was actually happening: I was steamrolling the people who had built those "ways of working." I thought I was helping, but I was actually taking away their control. It was humbling. Here's what I learned the hard way: sometimes you have to slow down to bring people with you. Otherwise you end up rowing in a circle. Alone. Now when I join a new team, I ask different questions: → Why was this built this way? → Who depends on this? → What does "better" actually mean to the people doing this work every day? Change still happens. It just happens with people, not to them. The fastest way forward is usually slower than you think 🚣
